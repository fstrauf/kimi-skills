# Interview guide (written podcast)

Companion for `article-conversation`. Technique only — not subject matter.

## Co-host principles

1. **Follow the energy** — chase what he lights up about; drop dead threads fast.
2. **One level deeper** — each question should add depth, not restart the topic.
3. **Concrete over clever** — prefer a moment, decision, or number over a slogan.
4. **Hold the tension** — good essays need trade-offs; pull both poles into view.
5. **Leave room for “I don’t know”** — uncertainty is often the honest ending.

## Question patterns (rotate; don’t run as a script)

### Open / seed
- What’s on your mind about this lately?
- If you had to explain it to a friend in two sentences, what would you say?
- What made this show up now rather than last year?

### Lived hook
- Is there a moment this stopped being abstract for you?
- What were you doing / where were you when it clicked?
- What’s a small example from the last few weeks?

### Sharpen the claim
- What’s the version of this idea you’d defend over a beer?
- What’s the version you’d reject (the strawman or the overclaim)?
- If someone summarised you wrong, what would they get wrong?

### Trade-offs
- What do you give up if you take this seriously?
- Who is this *not* for?
- When does this advice fail?

### Counterweight
- What’s the best argument against your view?
- What did you used to believe here?
- What would change your mind?

### Texture
- Any number, name, or detail that makes this real?
- What did you try that didn’t work?
- What still feels unresolved?

### Soft close material
- What do you want the reader to *feel*, not just understand?
- If the piece ends without a neat answer, what question should hang?
- What’s the quiet reframe in one line?

## Bad interview moves

- Firing five questions at once
- Leading him to a pre-written thesis
- Praising every answer (“Great insight!”) instead of thinking with him
- Turning every reply into a bullet outline
- Filling silence with your own essay
- Inventing examples “for him”

## Depth signals (enough to harvest)

You likely have enough when you can point to:

- [ ] A clear itch / why now
- [ ] At least one concrete hook *he* provided
- [ ] A plain-language claim in roughly his words
- [ ] Real tension or trade-off
- [ ] A natural soft-close direction

If any box is empty, one more good question beats a thin draft.

## Turn template

```
[Brief mirror: 1–2 sentences, his language]
[One deeper question]
[Optional: “If a concrete example comes to mind, even a small one, that helps.”]
```

## From talk to prose (later, in DRAFT)

- Keep his distinctive phrases when they land
- Cut false starts and repeated throat-clearing
- Keep spoken rhythm (short paragraphs, mid-thought precision)
- Never upgrade plain words into “content marketing”
- If a fact wasn’t said, leave it out or mark it as needing his input
